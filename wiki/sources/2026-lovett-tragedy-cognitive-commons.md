# The Tragedy of the Cognitive Commons: How AI Could Disrupt the Regeneration of Professional Expertise

**Citation.** Lovett, N. (2026). The tragedy of the cognitive commons: How AI could disrupt the regeneration of professional expertise. *Human Resource Development Review*. Advance online publication. https://doi.org/10.1177/15344843261470602

**Summary.** This peer-reviewed conceptual article frames professional expertise as a collective cognitive commons that organizations can deplete when AI substitutes for entry-level developmental work while externalizing the long-run cost of rebuilding expert capacity. It distinguishes Internalized Mastery from Distributed Mastery and argues that reliable substantive AI validation depends on the former even when the latter can produce plausible output. The paper proposes occupational vulnerability factors, governance hypotheses, and an empirical research agenda; it does not demonstrate profession-level depletion.

## Key claims
- The Cognitive Commons is the collective pool of deep professional expertise that is regenerated through sustained practice and developmental pathways but can be degraded when firms independently remove the work through which novices acquire independent judgment.
- Internalized Mastery is deep domain knowledge and diagnostic judgment developed through sustained practice, whereas Distributed Mastery is domain-specific proficiency in orchestrating human–AI workflows; the latter does not by itself establish the former.
- The proposed Validation Tether holds that substantive detection of plausible-but-wrong AI outputs requires Internalized Mastery, so systems may retain nominal human review while losing the expert capacity that makes review meaningful.
- The framework predicts greater commons vulnerability where developmental tasks are highly substitutable and modularized and where regulatory intensity, safety criticality, and professional-association strength provide weaker counterpressures.
- The article proposes organizational, professional-association, and policy-level governance options, including protected developmental stages and supervised-practice infrastructure, as hypotheses to test rather than established interventions.

## Evidence & limitations
- Peer-reviewed conceptual article, published online 2026-07-26 in *Human Resource Development Review*; the ingested PDF is the author accepted manuscript, which identifies the DOI version of record as the preferred citation.
- The framework synthesizes commons theory, HRD, distributed cognition, and selected early labor-market, clinical, and experimental findings; its profession-level depletion mechanism and governance interventions are falsifiable predictions, not observed outcomes.
- Proposed indicators include cohort-specific entry-level employment, unaided task performance, detection of seeded plausible errors, and comparative studies of professional-association governance, but these are not validated measurement instruments.
