# AI-Washing and the Phantom Productivity Paradox: When Anticipated Automation Drives Real Workforce Reductions

**Citation.** Westover, J. H. (2026). AI-Washing and the Phantom Productivity Paradox: When Anticipated Automation Drives Real Workforce Reductions. *Human Capital Leadership Review, 33*(2). https://doi.org/10.70175/hclreview.2020.33.2.2

**Summary.** This practitioner article argues that organizations can invoke anticipated AI capability to justify workforce reductions before systems demonstrably replace the affected work, calling the pattern “AI-washing.” It synthesizes labor-market reporting, organizational-behavior literature, and illustrative cases to argue that premature AI-attributed cuts can erode organizational knowledge and trust while weakening the learning needed for effective AI adoption. It recommends performance-based deployment gates, transparent communication, employee participation, capability building, and support for workers affected by genuine displacement.

## Key claims
- The article defines AI-washing in workforce restructuring as attributing layoffs or role reductions to AI systems that are underdeployed, underperforming, or tangential to the eliminated roles, with temporal mismatch, capability gaps, and later attribution changes offered as diagnostic features.
- It cites business-press reporting of approximately 55,000 U.S. job cuts attributed to AI in 2024 versus roughly 4,000 in 2023, while cautioning that attribution data and deployment measures do not establish the scale of AI-washing.
- It argues that workforce reductions made before functional substitution can remove tacit knowledge, lower trust and psychological safety, and constrain later innovation and AI implementation; these causal implications are synthesized from prior literature and illustrative cases rather than tested by a new empirical study.
- It recommends that organizations tie replacement decisions to task-relevant performance benchmarks, human baselines, pilot evidence, stakeholder review, monitoring, and reversal conditions rather than to aspirational AI narratives.

## Evidence & limitations
- Practitioner article that synthesizes prior research, labor-market reporting, and illustrative corporate cases; it does not report an original sample, causal design, or systematic content analysis of layoff announcements.
- The cited 2024 AI-attributed-cut figure concerns stated causes in public reporting, not verified technical substitution or realized employment displacement; the article’s AI-washing diagnosis should therefore be treated as a framework and hypothesis for evidence collection, not a prevalence estimate.
- The article is publicly available through its DOI landing page and is classified as other rather than peer-reviewed.
