# AI-accelerated End-to-End Framework for Rapid Professional Upskilling

**Citation.** Nguyen, T., Nguyen, H., & Ogburn, R. (2026). *AI-accelerated End-to-End Framework for Rapid Professional Upskilling*. arXiv. https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2607.14044

**Summary.** This preprint describes a five-stage, AI-accelerated pipeline for professional upskilling: knowledge acquisition, content development, content review and verification, AI-tutor coaching, and assessment development. The authors distinguish production efficiency from learning efficiency and retain human judgment in blueprint design, SME review, misconception authoring, and item rating. Its evidence is a single early program implementation with external signals rather than a controlled comparison of learning outcomes.

## Key claims
- The framework pairs AI acceleration with stage-specific learning and quality mechanisms, including prerequisite ordering, fixed content templates, claim-level verification, protocolized tutoring, and blueprint-tagged assessment development.
- The implementation produced an approximately 3,000-page knowledge base, a 530-question assessment bank aligned to a 10-domain, 53-skill blueprint, three 100-question mock exams, 96 chapter quizzes, and ten 70-question simulated practice tests.
- Three learners who used the knowledge base alone passed NVIDIA's Certified Professional in Agentic AI examination; the authors explicitly treat this 3/3 result as an observed signal rather than a generalizable pass rate.
- The paper reports mixed internal quality checks: platform accuracy scored 95% on a 20-example sample and cross-chapter integrity found no invalid references among 268 checked, while only 5 of 10 chapters met the stated pedagogical-progression threshold at that time.
- NASBA approved a program built on the framework for continuing-professional-education credits, which the authors present as external accreditation of program design and delivery rather than proof of comparative learning effectiveness.

## Evidence & limitations
- Design-based framework paper and early implementation report; it is an arXiv preprint, not peer reviewed, and does not include a control group, representative learner sample, or longitudinal transfer evidence.
- The certification outcome is n=3; the authors do not estimate a population pass rate, and 14 additional learners were still progressing at the time of the paper.
- Several validation signals are reported by the authors and are not independently audited in the paper; fully specified SME sign-off was not yet fully evidenced in execution records.
- Public provenance verified: the ingested Drive PDF exactly matches the public arXiv PDF by SHA-256. The arXiv API returned HTTP 429 during preflight, and Crossref title search did not identify a matching published record.
