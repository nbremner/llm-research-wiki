# The Crowdless Future? Generative AI and Creative Problem-Solving

**Citation.** Boussioux, L., Lane, J. N., Zhang, M., Jacimovic, V., & Lakhani, K. R. (2024). The Crowdless Future? Generative AI and Creative Problem-Solving. *Organization Science, 35*(5), 1589–1607. https://doi.org/10.1287/orsc.2023.18430

**Summary.** This field study compares human-crowd and GPT-4-assisted solution search for circular-economy business ideas, using 300 external evaluators and 3,900 evaluator-solution ratings. Human–AI solutions had lower average novelty but higher average strategic, environmental, and financial value and higher overall quality than crowd solutions. Iterative differentiation prompts to a single GPT-4 instance improved novelty relative to independent multiple-instance generation without compromising perceived value.

## Key claims
- Across the study's evaluated solutions, human–AI outputs were rated lower on novelty but higher on strategic viability, environmental value, financial value, and overall quality than human-crowd outputs.
- Human-crowd solutions were more likely than human–AI solutions to receive top novelty ratings, so higher average AI-assisted value did not eliminate the crowd's advantage at the novelty frontier.
- A single-instance, iteratively prompted GPT-4 configuration produced higher novelty, strategic viability, and overall quality than multiple independent GPT-4 instances, indicating that human guidance of search diversity changes creative-output performance.
- The final human–AI solutions were generated in 5.5 hours for $27.01, compared with 2,520 hours and $2,555 for the crowd solutions in this study.

## Evidence & limitations
- The study evaluated 234 randomly selected human-crowd and human–AI solutions in a circular-economy challenge; 300 U.S.-based external evaluators rated blinded solution blocks, and the analysis used nested mixed-effects models across 3,900 evaluator-solution pairs.
- Findings concern GPT-4 configurations and prompting practices used in 2023, one creative-problem domain, non-expert evaluators, and outcomes rated for novelty and value rather than observed implementation performance.
- This is a peer-reviewed Organization Science article; its human–AI results reflect the study's prompt engineer, model settings, and selected solution-quality criteria rather than a general claim that AI replaces human creative search.
