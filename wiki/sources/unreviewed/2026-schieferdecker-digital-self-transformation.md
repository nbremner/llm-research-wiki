# When Digitalization Transforms Itself: AI, Software, and the Next Technical Order

**Citation.** Schieferdecker, I. K. (2026). *When Digitalization Transforms Itself: AI, Software, and the Next Technical Order* (arXiv:2609.15350).

**Summary.** This conceptual preprint argues that agentic AI makes digitalization recursive by applying AI to the development, testing, operation, and evolution of digital systems, especially software engineering. It contends that automating portions of implementation shifts scarce human work toward defining system purpose, architecture, quality goals, verification and validation, security, traceability, and accountability. The chapter frames the ability to design, verify, and evolve digital systems as a strategic condition for digital sovereignty, with particular attention to Germany and Europe.

## Key claims
- Agentic systems can increasingly pursue multi-step software-engineering tasks, operate development environments, modify software, run tests, and iteratively improve results, moving automation beyond isolated code generation.
- As implementation work is automated, software engineering becomes more rather than less consequential because humans must specify intentions and quality boundaries and govern verification, traceability, and accountability.
- Lower marginal costs for some forms of software creation do not automatically yield productivity gains; the author argues that complementary investments in organizational processes, competencies, trust infrastructures, and independent evidence remain necessary.
- Agentic software production can create infrastructure and vendor dependencies, so the capacity to understand, design, verify, and evolve systems is presented as a condition for preserving strategic choice.

## Evidence & limitations
- This is a conceptual, policy-oriented chapter/preprint rather than a new empirical study or causal evaluation; its claims synthesize technical, economic, and governance arguments.
