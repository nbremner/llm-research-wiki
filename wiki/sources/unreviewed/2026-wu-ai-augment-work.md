# When Does AI Augment Work? A Workflow-Level Framework for Human-Agent Collaboration

**Citation.** Wu, J., Ziems, C., Chan, R., Chen, N. F., Chua, C., Chung, G., Hahn, J., Lee, W. S., Liu, Z., Ng, J., Ong, D. C., Ong, J., Soon, D. R., Song, T., Tan, Z.-X., Tao, S., Yang, E., Yang, Y., Yin, S. X., Kan, M.-Y., & Yang, D. (2026). *When Does AI Augment Work? A Workflow-Level Framework for Human-Agent Collaboration*. arXiv:2609.12482.

**Summary.** This CIVIC-AI workshop whitepaper proposes evaluating AI augmentation at the workflow level rather than through adoption, productivity, or headcount measures alone. It defines six conditions for genuine augmentation: durable net value, meaningful human control, clear accountability and recovery, deepening learning, preserved career pathways, and job purpose. A social-survey case study illustrates allocating bounded and reviewable execution to an agent while retaining human authority over purpose, validity, interpretation, and consequential use.

## Key claims
- Apparent AI productivity gains do not establish durable net value unless evaluation includes human review, exception handling, rework, recovery, and cognitive burden shifted elsewhere in the workflow.
- Meaningful human control requires workers to have the competence, time, information, and authority to detect failures, challenge assumptions, constrain actions, override decisions, and continue work during system unavailability.
- Workflow evaluation should distinguish snapshot integrity conditions from longitudinal effects on learning, career pathways, agency, and job purpose because delegation can remove the formative tasks needed for later human oversight.
- Delegation is more appropriate when outputs are inspectable, errors reversible, and stakes bounded; humans should retain stronger authority over goals, validity, ambiguous evidence, and consequential use.

## Evidence & limitations
- This is a 2026 workshop-derived whitepaper that advances a normative framework and one worked social-survey example rather than reporting a controlled empirical evaluation of the six conditions.
