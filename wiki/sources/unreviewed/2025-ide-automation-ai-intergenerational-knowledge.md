# Automation, AI, and the Intergenerational Transmission of Knowledge

**Citation.** Ide, E. (2025). *Automation, AI, and the Intergenerational Transmission of Knowledge.* SSRN working paper. https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.5360803

**Summary.** This working paper develops a task-based overlapping-generations model in which novices acquire tacit knowledge by working with experts. It argues that entry-level automation can increase immediate output while weakening long-run growth and welfare if it reallocates novices away from the most productive mentors. The countervailing case is automation that expands the number of novices learning from highly productive experts.

## Key claims
- Entry-level automation can raise output at adoption while lowering subsequent growth and welfare, even when it does not reduce entry-level employment, by worsening the composition of novice-expert matches.
- In the model, slower diffusion of best practices follows when automation shifts novices from the most productive experts to lower-quality mentors.
- Technological changes that increase the number of novices working with the most productive experts strengthen tacit-knowledge transmission and long-run growth.

## Evidence & limitations
- The paper is a theoretical task-based overlapping-generations model rather than a causal empirical study; its conclusions depend on assumptions about tacit knowledge, incomplete contracts, and mentor matching.
- The ingested Drive PDF exactly matches the public arXiv version (2507.16078); the canonical DOI is a 2025 SSRN working-paper record, so the work is not treated as peer-reviewed.
