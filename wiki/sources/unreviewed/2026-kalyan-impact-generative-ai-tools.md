# Impact of Generative AI Tools on Employee Productivity,Job Satisfaction, and Workforce Engagement: A Study of Knowledge-Based Organizations

**Citation.** Kalyan, P., Ramulu, P. J., & Mudem, P. (2026). Impact of Generative AI Tools on Employee Productivity,Job Satisfaction, and Workforce Engagement: A Study of Knowledge-Based Organizations. *International Journal of AI Electrical Civil and Mechanical Engineering, 2*(3), 400–407. https://doi.org/10.64751/8cq5ay88

**Summary.** This article reports a convenience-sample survey of 120 employees in knowledge-intensive sectors about their perceptions of generative-AI use, productivity, job satisfaction, and workforce engagement. The authors describe generally favorable associations, but the reported results are descriptive self-reports rather than observed performance outcomes or a documented causal design. Its strongest use is as limited perceptual evidence about AI as a possible job resource, alongside stated concerns about privacy, ethical use, displacement, and reskilling.

## Key claims
- Of 120 respondents, 100 (83.3%) agreed or strongly agreed that AI improves productivity, while 94 (78.3%) gave the same response for increased job satisfaction and workforce engagement.
- The survey used five-point Likert-scale perceptions of AI adoption and workplace outcomes among respondents selected through convenience sampling because they used or knew about workplace generative-AI tools.
- The authors attribute favorable perceptions to reduced routine work, improved flexibility and learning opportunities, and AI-enabled collaboration and knowledge sharing, while also reporting concerns about privacy, ethical use, job displacement, and continuous upskilling.

## Evidence & limitations
- The article describes a quantitative questionnaire study spanning IT, consulting, finance, education, healthcare, and research, with descriptive statistics plus stated correlation and multiple-regression analyses.
- The reported results present agreement counts and percentages but do not document regression coefficients, sampling frame, geographic context, response rate, validated measures, or observed productivity; causal and generalizable claims should therefore be treated cautiously.
