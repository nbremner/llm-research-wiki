# Artificial intelligence, machine learning, and big data: Improvements to the science of people at work and applications to practice

**Citation.** Woo, S. E., Tay, L., & Oswald, F. (2024). Artificial intelligence, machine learning, and big data: Improvements to the science of people at work and applications to practice. *Personnel Psychology, 77*(4), 1387–1402. https://doi.org/10.1111/peps.12643

**Summary.** This editorial introduces a *Personnel Psychology* special issue on AI, machine learning, and big data in organizational science. It argues that these methods can advance theory and knowledge, construct measurement, and prediction, while requiring attention to data access, methodological capability, transparency, privacy, reproducibility, generalizability, and interpretability. The article is a conceptual overview rather than new empirical evidence.

## Key claims
- AI/ML and big-data methods can broaden organizational research through inductive and abductive theorizing, richer behavioral and temporal data, new measurement modes, and prediction of organizational outcomes.
- AI/ML-derived scores representing constructs of theoretical interest still require fundamental psychometric evidence for reliability, validity, and fairness; model sophistication does not substitute for construct interpretation.
- Organizational big-data research faces practical and institutional constraints, including restricted data access, privacy and confidentiality risks, computational and methodological skill gaps, limited reproducibility, uncertain generalizability, and difficulty interpreting complex models.
- The authors recommend AI/ML methodological literacy and multidisciplinary collaboration, including prospective work with organizational partners so data are intentionally collected rather than merely incidental.

## Evidence & limitations
- Peer-reviewed editorial for a special issue; it synthesizes examples and recommendations rather than testing a new dataset or causal model.
