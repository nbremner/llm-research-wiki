# Just reallocated? Robots displacement, and job quality

**Citation.** Cuccu, L. & Royuela, V. (2024). Just reallocated? Robots displacement, and job quality. *British Journal of Industrial Relations, 62*(4), 705–731. https://doi.org/10.1111/bjir.12805

**Summary.** This study examines whether workers displaced from Spanish sectors with rising industrial-robot density obtain comparable re-employment rather than merely leaving unemployment. In a longitudinal administrative panel covering 2001–2017, robot exposure is associated with worse six-month re-employment prospects and lower-quality new matches for middle- and low-skilled displaced workers. The penalties are especially pronounced when workers reallocate across sectors, while high-skilled workers are less adversely affected but can still incur sector-change penalties.

## Key claims
- For middle- and low-skilled displaced workers, greater robot exposure is associated with a higher probability of remaining unemployed six months after displacement than for non-exposed workers.
- Among re-employed middle- and low-skilled workers, one additional industrial robot per 1,000 workers increases the probability of moving to a lower-paying job by about 1.9 percentage points.
- Reallocation across sectors carries substantially larger job-quality penalties than re-employment within a sector; exposed workers also show qualification downgrading and greater use of temporary-employment agencies.
- High-skilled workers are less negatively affected by robot exposure, although changing sectors can still impose a re-employment penalty.

## Evidence & limitations
- The analysis uses a large longitudinal administrative panel of Spanish workers from 2001–2017 and measures exposure through increasing industrial-robot density in displaced workers' sectors.
- The evidence concerns industrial robots, displacement, and subsequent job matches in Spain; its estimates may not transfer directly to generative AI, other automation technologies, other labor-market institutions, or non-displaced workers.
- This is a peer-reviewed journal article; the record is not yet human-reviewed for synthesis.
