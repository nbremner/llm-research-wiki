# AI-powered Automatic Item Generation for Psychological Tests: A Conceptual Framework for an LLM-based Multi-Agent AIG System

**Citation.** Lee, P., Son, M., & Jia, Z. (2025). AI-powered automatic item generation for psychological tests: A conceptual framework for an LLM-based multi-agent AIG system. *Journal of Business and Psychology, 41*(1), 71–99. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10869-025-10067-y

**Summary.** This peer-reviewed article proposes an LLM-based multi-agent system for generating and reviewing psychological-test items, with specialized roles for item writing, content review, linguistic review, bias review, and meta-editing that incorporates human feedback. In a demonstration generating 24 items across six dimensions of attitudes toward AI at work, 21 graduate I-O psychology raters evaluated AI-generated and human-authored items while blinded to source. The authors present the framework as a way to embed psychometric quality control in the item-development process rather than relying only on post hoc review.

## Key claims
- The LM-AIG framework separates item generation from content, linguistic, and bias review, then uses a meta-editor to synthesize agent feedback and human expert input before final item selection.
- In the demonstration, 21 of 24 AI-generated items were judged comparable in quality to human-authored items; the authors report that three items required particular attention and that human feedback remained especially important for content validity.
- Human raters found strong content-validity results for AI-generated items in four of six attitude dimensions, while AI-use anxiety and perceived adaptability showed lower substantive-agreement and substantive-validity indices than the human-authored comparison items.
- The authors argue that agent-based review can make item feedback more actionable than aggregate classification accuracy alone by evaluating construct correspondence, distinctiveness, readability, contextual specificity, and demographic bias.

## Evidence & limitations
- The empirical demonstration used 21 graduate students in an I-O psychology program to rate 24 generated and 24 human-authored items for one workplace-AI attitude instrument; raters were blinded to item source and item order was randomized.
- This is evidence about a bounded item-generation demonstration, not validation of a finished selection instrument, live employment decisions, criterion-related validity, measurement invariance, or the framework's comparative advantage over other AIG workflows.
- The article is peer-reviewed, published in *Journal of Business and Psychology*, and distributed under CC BY 4.0; public provenance was confirmed through the title-page DOI and an exact-title Crossref match.
