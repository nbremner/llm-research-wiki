# The Network Structure of Big Tech's Inter-Organizational Collaboration on Generative AI

**Citation.** Isada, F. (2025). The Network Structure of Big Tech's Inter-Organizational Collaboration on Generative AI. *ENTRENOVA - ENTerprise REsearch InNOVAtion, 11*(1). https://doi.org/10.54820/entrenova-2025-0010

**Summary.** This study uses social-network analysis of 2024 news and press-release data to compare Big Tech firms' positions in inter-organizational generative-AI collaboration networks with those of other organizations. It finds that the examined Big Tech firms have substantially larger, less constrained, more intermediary, and sparser ego networks, consistent with ecosystem-broker roles. The source describes the network as concentrated around a small number of firms while noting that the structure may change as models and applications diversify.

## Key claims
- Across 3,239 articles and press releases, the study identified 1,223 organizations and 22,814 collaboration pairs; the overall mean number of distinct organizational connections was 2.87.
- Microsoft had 157 identified collaborators, followed by AWS (73) and Google (59); the sampled Big Tech organizations had a mean network size of 52.00 versus 2.54 for other organizations (Welch test, p=.020).
- Compared with other organizations, the Big Tech group had lower constraint (0.20 versus 0.95), higher normalized ego betweenness (84.30 versus 10.10), and lower ego-network density (8.06 versus 88.98); all reported differences were significant at the 5% level.

## Evidence & limitations
- The analysis extracts collaboration relationships from 2024 Dow Jones newspaper coverage and press releases, then applies manual review and UCINet social-network measures to organization pairs.
- News and press-release coverage can reflect unequal collection and editorial practices, the network is unweighted, and the author notes that information unavailable from newspaper articles requires other methods.
