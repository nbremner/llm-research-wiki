# Generative AI in Organizational Knowledge Management – AI literacy as a prerequisite for augmentation

**Citation.** Wilkens, U., Langholf, V., & Obermann, N. (2026). *Generative AI in Organizational Knowledge Management – AI literacy as a prerequisite for augmentation*. *Industry 4.0 Science, 42*(5), 118.

**Summary.** This article uses a qualitative case study at a pump manufacturer to examine how GenAI-supported knowledge management can combine codified, firm-specific, and employees’ tacit knowledge in industrial maintenance. It argues that process augmentation and workforce augmentation co-occur only when AI literacy develops alongside domain expertise, rather than replacing it. The authors distinguish augmented, decoupled, and loss-of-expertise user patterns and position critical reflection and ethical judgment as necessary parts of AI literacy.

## Key claims
- GenAI-supported knowledge management must address machine documentation, firm-specific routines, formal employee qualifications, and difficult-to-codify experiential knowledge rather than treating organizational knowledge as a single repository.
- In two rounds of qualitative interviews with twelve employees each, the case found that employees used AI agents to automate recurring multi-step workflows and departments developed specialized agents around local problems.
- The article identifies three user patterns: an augmented type that links AI literacy to expanding domain capability, a decoupled type that confines AI to peripheral work, and a loss-of-expertise type concerned that AI use weakens professional competence.
- Joint process and workforce augmentation requires user competence, foundational understanding of GenAI, critical-reflective skill, ethical judgment, and domain expertise to develop together.

## Evidence & limitations
- The evidence is a longitudinal qualitative thematic analysis in one medium-sized pump manufacturer, based on self-reported user interaction during GenAI implementation; it supports mechanism-building rather than prevalence claims.
- The authors state that claims about the frequency of augmentation and user types require quantitative validation, and the industrial-maintenance setting may not transfer directly to other knowledge-work settings.
