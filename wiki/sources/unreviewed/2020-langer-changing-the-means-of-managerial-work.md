# Changing the means of managerial work: effects of automated decision support systems on personnel selection tasks

**Citation.** Langer, M., König, C. J., & Busch, V. (2021). Changing the means of managerial work: Effects of automated decision support systems on personnel selection tasks. *Journal of Business and Psychology, 36*(5), 751–769. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10869-020-09711-6

**Summary.** This laboratory experiment examined how the timing of automated applicant-ranking support changes a simulated personnel-selection task. Among 122 participants completing five rounds, support shown after participants first reviewed applicant information produced greater decision satisfaction and steeper gains in selection self-efficacy than support shown before processing or no support, without significant performance or efficiency benefits.

## Key claims
- Automated decision support can change managerial task experiences even when it does not significantly improve selection-task performance or decision speed.
- Showing an automated ranking after initial human information processing produced higher decision satisfaction than the other conditions.
- Support-after-processing produced a steeper increase in personnel-selection self-efficacy than support-before-processing or no support.

## Evidence & limitations
- A preregistered three-condition laboratory experiment assigned 122 German participants to support-before-processing, support-after-processing, or no-support versions of a five-round simulated personnel-selection task.
- The simulated task had no time pressure and participants quickly approached a performance ceiling, so the null performance and efficiency findings warrant caution and may not generalize to practicing hiring managers or higher-stakes selection settings.
