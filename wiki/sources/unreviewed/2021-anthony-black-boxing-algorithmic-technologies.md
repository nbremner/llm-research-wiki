# When Knowledge Work and Analytical Technologies Collide: The Practices and Consequences of Black Boxing Algorithmic Technologies

**Citation.** Anthony, C. (2021). When knowledge work and analytical technologies collide: The practices and consequences of black boxing algorithmic technologies. *Administrative Science Quarterly, 66*(4), 1173–1212. https://doi.org/10.1177/00018392211016755

**Summary.** Anthony reports a two-year inductive ethnography of four investment-bank groups using analytical technologies to validate financial analyses. The study distinguishes partitioning practices, in which junior staff assemble analyses and senior staff interpret them, from co-construction practices that involve junior interpretation and senior feedback. Partitioning was associated with routine black boxing of embedded algorithms and constrained junior expertise development, whereas co-construction was associated with greater awareness of algorithms and their possible effects.

## Key claims
- Validating an analysis through organizational practices does not necessarily entail understanding the calculations performed by the analytical technology.
- In partitioning groups, junior bankers assembled work with analytical tools while senior bankers interpreted it; both groups routinely took embedded algorithms for granted without understanding them.
- In co-construction groups, junior bankers interpreted analyses and senior bankers provided feedback on their reasoning and choices; participants were more conscious of embedded algorithms and their potential impact.
- Black boxing shaped knowledge outputs and constrained junior members' expertise development, with consequences for career trajectories.

## Evidence & limitations
- Two-year inductive ethnographic study across four groups in one investment bank; the evidence supports process explanations rather than population-wide causal estimates.
- The setting concerns financial analytical technologies and hierarchical knowledge work before contemporary generative AI, so transfer to other tools or organizations requires caution.
