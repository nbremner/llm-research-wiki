# Accountability Asymmetry and Structural Trust in Autonomous AI Systems

**Citation.** DeBardeleben, N. (2026). *Accountability Asymmetry and Structural Trust in Autonomous AI Systems*. arXiv:2608.03670.

**Summary.** This preprint argues that delegating operational authority to autonomous AI creates accountability asymmetry: consequences for bad decisions fall on deploying people and institutions rather than on the system selecting the action. It frames governance as an infrastructure-reliability problem and proposes engineered heterogeneity, in which the action proposer is not its sole approver or auditor. The paper is conceptual and uses scientific-computing infrastructure to illustrate how authority, reversibility, observability, and review should be designed together.

## Key claims
- Alignment and organizational liability can improve deployment behavior but do not reproduce the pre-action deterrent that constrains human operators whose own futures are at stake.
- Governance should scale with an autonomous system's operational authority, possible harm, uncertainty, and reversibility rather than treating all AI assistance as equivalent.
- Structural trust requires separating proposal, approval, execution, monitoring, and later audit so that the component initiating an action is not the only process able to validate it.
- Long-horizon audit trails are needed to detect gradual authority expansion or declining observability that may not be evident in a single action.

## Evidence & limitations
- Conceptual governance paper grounded in illustrative scenarios from scientific-computing infrastructure; it does not report an empirical evaluation of the proposed controls.
- Public provenance verified against arXiv metadata and the public arXiv PDF; the public PDF exactly matches the ingested Drive artifact by SHA-256.
- Preprint; its proposed accountability and engineered-heterogeneity framework requires empirical and comparative validation across deployment settings.
