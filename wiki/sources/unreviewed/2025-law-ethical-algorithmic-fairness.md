# The ethical imperative of algorithmic fairness in AI-enabled hiring: a critical analysis of bias, accountability, and justice

**Citation.** Law, J. (2025). The ethical imperative of algorithmic fairness in AI-enabled hiring: a critical analysis of bias, accountability, and justice. *AI and Ethics, 6*, 65. https://doi.org/10.1007/s43681-025-00927-x

**Summary.** This peer-reviewed normative analysis argues that technical fairness interventions and compliance frameworks in AI-enabled hiring may reduce measurable disparities without addressing equitable access to opportunity, recognition, candidate dignity, or procedural fairness. It proposes an ethical-by-design governance model that combines justice, capability, and recognition principles with technical implementation and accountability.

## Key claims
- Technical mitigation and regulatory compliance can reduce measurable disparities while leaving structural inequities, constrained opportunity, devaluation of marginalized groups, and procedural unfairness unaddressed.
- A compliance-centered approach can enable “bias washing,” in which formal fairness requirements are met without substantive equity.
- An ethical-by-design governance model should join normative principles to technical implementation so hiring systems distribute opportunity more equitably and uphold candidate dignity.

## Evidence & limitations
- This is a critical normative analysis synthesizing prior empirical and legal literature rather than a new empirical study; its claims about intervention limits are interpretive and require empirical evaluation in specific hiring systems.
