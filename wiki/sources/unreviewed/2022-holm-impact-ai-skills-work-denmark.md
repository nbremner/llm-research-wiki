# The impact of artificial intelligence on skills at work in Denmark

**Citation.** Holm, J. R., & Lorenz, E. (2022). The impact of artificial intelligence on skills at work in Denmark. *New Technology, Work and Employment, 37*(1), 79–101. https://doi.org/10.1111/ntwe.12215

**Summary.** Holm and Lorenz analyze 2019 Danish employee survey data to relate daily AI use to within-job skill requirements rather than inferring impacts from occupation-level exposure. They distinguish AI used as information for workers’ own decisions from AI that gives workers orders, finding that the associations with learning, autonomy, high-performance work practices, and work pace differ by use type and occupational skill group.

## Key claims
- For high-skill jobs, daily AI use as an input to employee decision making is associated with less monotony, more learning, more teamwork, task flexibility, and quality-control responsibility, but also greater work-pace constraints.
- Daily receipt of AI-generated orders is associated with greater work-pace constraints across skill groups; in high-skill jobs it is also associated with lower autonomy, while in mid-skill jobs it is associated with greater monotony and less learning.
- The reported positive skill and work-practice associations of decision-support AI are concentrated in high-skill jobs, while the adverse associations of AI-generated orders are especially pronounced for mid-skill jobs, a pattern the authors connect to labor-market inequality and job polarization.
- Additive-noise-model tests are consistent with AI use affecting several job-skill dimensions rather than the reverse, but the authors do not treat the cross-sectional survey as definitive causal identification.

## Evidence & limitations
- The study uses the weighted 2019 TASK survey of Danish employees outside public-sector administration in workplaces with at least five full-time-equivalent employees; the analyzed regressions report N = 1,116 and measure job requirements through workers’ reported tasks rather than self-rated capability.
- Five principal-component measures capture monotony, work-pace constraints, two high-performance-work-practice dimensions, and autonomy; the study contrasts daily AI-supported decision making with daily AI-generated orders.
- The survey is cross-sectional, cannot observe implementation processes or changes over time, and aggregates heterogeneous AI technologies into two broad employee-facing use types; the additive-noise causal analysis is therefore supportive rather than a substitute for longitudinal or experimental evidence.
