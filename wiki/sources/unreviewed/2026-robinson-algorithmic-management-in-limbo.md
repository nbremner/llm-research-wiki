# Algorithmic Management in Limbo: Task-Driven Interweaving of Hierarchy and Market Management

**Citation.** Robinson, A. P., Jarrahi, M. H., Keegan, A., & Meijerink, J. (2026). Algorithmic management in limbo: Task-driven interweaving of hierarchy and market management. *Human Resource Management, 65*(1), 117–131. https://doi.org/10.1002/hrm.70019

**Summary.** This inductive qualitative study examines how 23 digital labor platforms use algorithmic management across HR activities, drawing on 33 semi-structured interviews with platform workers and platform documentary data. It finds that platform algorithms combine market-oriented matching and hierarchy-oriented control rather than operating as one or the other, with the balance shaped by task characteristics.

## Key claims
- Digital labor platforms use algorithmic management for HR activities including worker selection, task assignment, performance management, supervision, and remuneration.
- Task autonomy, complexity, and interdependence are associated with more market-oriented algorithmic management, while urgency is associated with more hierarchy-oriented management.
- For routine, time-sensitive, and tightly specified tasks, platforms tend to automate matching and control through hierarchy-oriented mechanisms that reduce discretion and enforce predictable execution.
- For complex or creative tasks requiring autonomy or client interaction, platforms tend to use market-oriented mechanisms that permit more worker discretion and flexible matching.
- The study argues that digital labor platforms occupy a conditional "limbo" between market intermediary and organizational supervisor, using algorithms to shift between flexibility and control.

## Evidence & limitations
- Inductive qualitative analysis of 33 platform-worker interviews and documentary data from 23 digital labor platforms supports a task-centered framework rather than causal estimates of algorithmic effects.
- The framework is based on platform work and may not transfer unchanged to conventional employment relationships or non-platform AI management systems.
- Published open access in *Human Resource Management*; the record uses the public DOI landing page as canonical provenance.
