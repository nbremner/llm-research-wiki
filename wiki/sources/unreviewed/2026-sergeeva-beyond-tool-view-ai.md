# Beyond the tool view of AI: Intelligent technologies and the emergence of new epistemic regimes

**Citation.** Sergeeva, A. V., Leonardi, P. M., & Faraj, S. (2026). *Beyond the tool view of AI: Intelligent technologies and the emergence of new epistemic regimes*. *Strategic Organization*. https://doi.org/10.1177/14761270261436479

**Summary.** This conceptual special-issue editorial argues that intelligent technologies should be understood not merely as tools adopted, automated, or augmented within existing organizations, but as alternative bases for making knowledge claims. It introduces an epistemic-regime lens for analyzing contests over credible evidence, legitimate expertise, and decision authority when computational outputs gain standing. The article develops a research agenda on changes in epistemic authority, valuation criteria, and the sociotechnical actors that produce organizational knowledge.

## Key claims
- The authors argue that AI outputs grounded in computational modeling, statistical inference, and large-scale data infrastructures can contest organizational knowledge claims grounded in professional experience, disciplinary training, and situated judgment.
- The epistemic-regime lens shifts analysis from information processing or tool adoption to the institutional arrangements that determine what counts as credible knowledge, who may interpret it, and how decisions are justified.
- AI can reconfigure epistemic authority by making demonstrable outputs, statistical validation, and scalable computation competing bases for recognized expertise alongside credentials and experiential knowledge.
- The article identifies three research directions: the redistribution of epistemic authority, the institutionalization of computational valuation schemes such as speed and measurable accuracy, and the emergence of sociotechnical epistemic subjects whose accountability cannot be assigned solely to an individual human or an AI system.

## Evidence & limitations
- This is a conceptual editorial in the peer-reviewed journal *Strategic Organization* that synthesizes organization and strategy theory to propose a research agenda; it does not report a new empirical study or causal estimates.
- Its claims are strongest as an organizing lens for studying authority, valuation, legitimacy, and accountability in AI-mediated organizing, rather than as evidence that any specific organizational outcome will occur.
- The article focuses on organization and strategy theory, so its framework requires empirical testing across domains, professional settings, and forms of intelligent technology.
