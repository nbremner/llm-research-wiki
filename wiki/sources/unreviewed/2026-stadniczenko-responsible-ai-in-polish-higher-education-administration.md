# Responsible AI in Polish Higher-Education Administration under the EU AI Act: High-Risk Use Cases, Fundamental-Rights Impact Assessment, and Institutional Accountability

**Citation.** Stadniczeńko, D. (2026). Responsible AI in Polish higher-education administration under the EU AI Act: High-risk use cases, fundamental-rights impact assessment, and institutional accountability. *AI, Law, Politics*, 2(S1), 82–112. https://doi.org/10.5709/alp-02.s1.2026-04

**Summary.** This doctrinal and legal-design article maps how Polish higher-education institutions should classify and govern administrative AI under the EU AI Act, GDPR, and relevant Polish law. It proposes a use-case classification matrix, a FRIA–DPIA boundary map, a four-gate institutional model, and an accountability file spanning procurement through retirement. The framework treats meaningful human authority, intelligible reasons, contestability, accessibility, and auditable institutional ownership as conditions of responsible deployment.

## Key claims
- Admissions, learning-outcome evaluation, level determination, test monitoring, and employment uses may be Annex III high-risk uses, while narrow procedural tools require fact-specific workflow assessment.
- A formal human signature does not by itself prevent material AI influence; the relevant unit of analysis is the deployed workflow, including outputs, defaults, reviewer practice, legal consequence, and available remedy.
- AI Act fundamental-rights impact assessment and GDPR data-protection impact assessment have distinct triggers and functions, so a FRIA neither applies to every private university nor replaces a DPIA.
- AI emotion inference in educational institutions is generally prohibited, apart from medical or safety purposes, and relabeling it as engagement, attention, or stress detection does not change the functional analysis.

## Evidence & limitations
- The article uses doctrinal and legal-design analysis of legislation, case law, official guidance, and selected interdisciplinary scholarship available through 2026-08-11; its practical tools require future empirical validation.
- The analysis is specific to Polish higher education and an evolving EU AI Act implementation timeline, so its classifications and institutional recommendations require jurisdiction- and workflow-specific reassessment.
