# The Impact of Employees’ AI-Driven Stress on Workforce Productivity through AI Collaboration: The Moderating Role of Human Intelligence

**Citation.** Oh, S.-J. & Seo, Y.-W. (2026). The Impact of Employees’ AI-Driven Stress on Workforce Productivity through AI Collaboration: The Moderating Role of Human Intelligence. *Journal of the Korea Contents Association, 26*(6), 480–495. https://doi.org/10.5392/jkca.2026.26.06.480

**Summary.** This survey study distinguishes challenge from hindrance forms of AI-driven technostress and tests their pathways to work productivity through AI-collaboration working well-being and human–AI collaboration technology-learning anxiety. Among employees with workplace AI-use experience, challenge stress was associated with greater AI-collaboration well-being and productivity, whereas hindrance stress was associated with poorer well-being, greater learning anxiety, and lower productivity. Human intelligence positively moderated only the pathway from challenge stress to AI-collaboration working well-being.

## Key claims
- In PLS-SEM analyses of 350 employees with workplace AI-use experience, AI challenge technostress increased AI-collaboration working well-being and thereby improved work productivity.
- AI challenge technostress did not significantly reduce human–AI collaboration technology-learning anxiety.
- AI hindrance technostress decreased AI-collaboration working well-being, increased technology-learning anxiety, and significantly lowered work productivity.
- Human intelligence had a significant positive moderating effect only between AI challenge technostress and AI-collaboration working well-being.

## Evidence & limitations
- The article reports a cross-sectional PLS-SEM analysis of 350 employees who had used AI at work, so the modeled pathways support associations rather than causal effects.
