# Using Augmentation-Based AI Tool at Work: A Daily Investigation of Learning-Based Benefit and Challenge

**Citation.** Shao, Y., Huang, C., Song, Y., Wang, M., Song, Y. H., & Shao, R. (2024). Using augmentation-based AI tool at work: A daily investigation of learning-based benefit and challenge. *Journal of Management*. https://doi.org/10.1177/01492063241266503

**Summary.** This peer-reviewed daily-diary study examines how employees' within-day use of augmentation-based AI relates to knowledge gain, information overload, task performance, and recovery. The authors report countervailing associations: more frequent use was associated with greater knowledge gain and subsequently better end-of-day task performance, but it was also associated with information overload that impaired performance and recovery. Openness to experience and positive affect shaped these daily relationships.

## Key claims
- More frequent augmentation-based AI use during a workday was associated with greater knowledge gain and, subsequently, better task performance at the end of that day.
- More frequent augmentation-based AI use was also associated with information overload, which in turn was associated with impaired end-of-day task performance and recovery.
- Employee openness to experience and momentary positive affect moderated the reported daily effects of augmentation-based AI use.

## Evidence & limitations
- The study uses a daily-diary design to examine within-day employee experiences of augmentation-based AI, drawing on AI-augmentation research and cognitive-load theory.
- The available Markdown artifact contains the abstract and reference list but not the full methods, sample details, measures, effect sizes, or full results; this record therefore reports only the abstract-level findings and should not be read as causal evidence.
