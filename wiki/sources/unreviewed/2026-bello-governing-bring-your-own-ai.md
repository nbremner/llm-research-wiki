# Governing Bring Your Own AI: A Parameterized Maturity Model

**Citation.** Bello, D., & Hastings, J. (2026). *Governing Bring Your Own AI: A Parameterized Maturity Model* [Preprint]. arXiv. https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2609.05236

**Summary.** This preprint develops a five-level maturity model for governing employees' use of personal generative-AI accounts outside enterprise identity and security controls. It combines a systematic literature review with a deterministic, parameterized model of technical-control coverage, framework gaps, and residual risk; the authors argue that technical, governance, and human controls must be layered rather than relying on prohibition alone.

## Key claims
- In its curated corpus of 24 risk-eligible studies, data exposure/privacy was the most frequently coded BYOAI risk category (14 studies; 58%), followed by compliance/legal risk (11; 46%), governance drift/visibility (10; 42%), and hallucination/output risk (9; 38%).
- The maturity ladder progresses from a prohibition-only posture through awareness and guardrails to integrated and optimized governance, adding data, cloud-access, identity, accountability, verification, training, and reporting/learning controls across technical, governance, and human pillars.
- The parameterized technical model reports higher composite governance scores and lower residual framework gaps as control coverage increases, but these directional results follow from the model's specified mappings rather than from observed organizational outcomes.

## Evidence & limitations
- The review codes a curated 30-record corpus (24 research studies and 6 framework documents); it does not claim exhaustive search coverage, and one researcher performed the coding without inter-rater reliability assessment.
- This is an arXiv preprint, not peer-reviewed research; the proposed metrics use fixed literature-derived and assumed parameters, quantify only the technical pillar, and the incident mappings are retrospective and conditional rather than prevention tests.
