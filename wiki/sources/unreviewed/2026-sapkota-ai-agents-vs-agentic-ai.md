# AI Agents vs. Agentic AI: A Conceptual Taxonomy, Applications and Challenges

**Citation.** Sapkota, R., Roumeliotis, K. I., & Karkee, M. (2026). AI Agents vs. Agentic AI: A Conceptual Taxonomy, Applications and Challenges. *Information Fusion, 126*, 103599. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.inffus.2025.103599

**Summary.** This conceptual review distinguishes tool- and task-oriented AI agents from agentic AI systems characterized by coordinated multi-agent autonomy, dynamic task decomposition, and persistent memory. It maps applications and technical challenges across the two paradigms, including hallucination, brittleness, emergent behavior, and coordination failures. The paper offers design-oriented remedies such as retrieval augmentation, tool use, memory architectures, and causal modeling, rather than evidence about human or organizational outcomes.

## Key claims
- The review characterizes AI agents as modular LLM- or LIM-enabled systems for task-specific automation, while agentic AI coordinates multiple agents with dynamic decomposition, persistent memory, and greater autonomy.
- It contrasts AI-agent applications such as customer support, scheduling, and summarization with agentic-AI applications such as research automation, robotic coordination, and medical decision support.
- It identifies hallucination, prompt brittleness, limited planning, and weak causal reasoning as AI-agent limitations, and inter-agent misalignment, error propagation, emergent unpredictability, explainability, and scalability as added agentic-AI challenges.

## Evidence & limitations
- This is a broad conceptual and literature review, not a primary empirical study; its taxonomy and solution proposals do not establish causal effects in workplaces or organizations.

- Although the review discusses governance and applications, its contribution is predominantly technical systems architecture and implementation; its fit with this wiki's behavioral and organizational lens requires human review before synthesis.
