# Designing for human rights in AI

**Citation.** Aizenberg, E., & van den Hoven, J. (2020). Designing for human rights in AI. *Big Data & Society, 7*(2). https://doi.org/10.1177/2053951720949566

**Summary.** Aizenberg and van den Hoven propose a Design for Values roadmap for translating fundamental human rights into context-dependent AI design requirements through structured stakeholder engagement. Grounded in the EU Charter of Fundamental Rights, the framework treats dignity, freedom, equality, and solidarity as top-level values that can be progressively specified into socio-technical requirements.

## Key claims
- The paper proposes using dignity, freedom, equality, and solidarity as top-level requirements for AI design, with more specific values such as privacy, transparency, non-discrimination, and safety specified in context.
- Its Design for Values process combines conceptual investigations of stakeholders and values, empirical investigations of stakeholder needs and experiences, and technical investigations of system properties and trade-offs.
- Stakeholder participation is presented as necessary for translating abstract rights and values into design requirements that are meaningful for a particular AI application and its affected communities.
- The authors position the framework as complementary to human-rights and algorithmic impact assessments because it links identified risks and stakeholder needs to positive design choices and traceable requirements.
- The paper argues that entities introducing AI systems bear primary responsibility for human-rights-respecting design, while public institutions have roles in oversight, verification, and adjudication.

## Evidence & limitations
- This is a conceptual and normative journal article that synthesizes Design for Values, Value Sensitive Design, Participatory Design, human-rights due diligence, and AI-governance literature; it does not empirically test implementation outcomes.
- The framework is explicitly grounded in the EU Charter of Fundamental Rights, so its value hierarchy and application require contextual interpretation rather than being treated as a universal specification.
