# Scaffolding Human–AI Collaboration: A Field Experiment on Behavioral Protocols and Cognitive Reframing

**Citation.** Farach, A., Cambon, A., Tankelevitch, L., Hsueh, C., & Janssen, R. (2026). *Scaffolding Human–AI Collaboration: A Field Experiment on Behavioral Protocols and Cognitive Reframing*. arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08678

**Summary.** In a single-day field experiment with 388 Gap Inc. employees, Farach et al. compared a pair-level behavioral protocol for joint Copilot use with unstructured use and an individual-level thought-partner training with standard feature training. The mandated pair protocol was associated with lower LLM-graded quality and sharply lower document production; the individual training had a null pre-specified continuous quality result but a higher exploratory probability of a perfect score. The authors treat both results as bounded by differential attrition, an AM/PM session confound, and limitations of LLM grading.

## Key claims
- In Task A, pairs assigned to the Create-Out-Loud behavioral protocol scored 4.96 points lower on the 22-point LLM-graded document-quality scale than unstructured-use controls (10.68 vs. 15.63; 95% CI [-6.71, -3.20]; p < .001), with negative Lee bounds under worst-case selective attrition.
- The behavioral-protocol condition had substantially lower odds of producing a Task A document (OR = 0.12, 95% CI [0.04, 0.35]; p < .001); 26.8% of treatment pairs versus 4.1% of control pairs failed to produce one.
- In Task B, thought-partner training did not significantly improve the pre-specified continuous quality outcome (18.1 vs. 17.3; p = .223), which had a 68.1% ceiling rate, but an exploratory post-hoc binary model found higher odds of a perfect score (OR = 2.07, 95% CI [1.12, 3.83]; p = .022); its Lee bounds crossed zero.
- The training condition showed greater change in exploration-and-experimentation beliefs (BH-adjusted p = .013) and the composite belief score (BH-adjusted p = .047), but the authors' alternative ANCOVA specification was null, consistent with recovery from Task A carry-over effects rather than a clean training effect.

## Evidence & limitations
- Public arXiv v2 preprint reporting a randomized, two-arm field experiment at one Fortune 500 retailer; the public arXiv PDF exactly matches the ingested Drive binary (SHA-256: 6caa83c874b308ff3b7b8338470a75f3251ad28ebb9022329e96bf4814cf8359).
- The behavioral and cognitive interventions differ in unit of analysis, task structure, outcome saturation, attrition, and sequence, so the study does not cleanly establish a general behavioral-versus-cognitive scaffolding taxonomy.
- Treatment assignment was confounded with AM/PM session timing; treatment conditions also had higher attrition, and LLM-as-judge scores were strongly associated with word count for Task A.
- The extracted PDF contained one ordinary scholarly reference to large language models and no model-directed or ingest-manipulation instructions.
