# Making Agent-Mediated Contributions Governable: A Project-Level Governance Manifest for Open-Source AI Collaboration

**Citation.** Gao, J., Li, L., Guo, S., He, L., & Sun, X. (2026). *Making Agent-Mediated Contributions Governable: A Project-Level Governance Manifest for Open-Source AI Collaboration* (arXiv:2607.15769). https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2607.15769

**Summary.** This preprint argues that agent-readable repository instructions and provenance traces are insufficient for governing AI-mediated open-source contributions because projects also need contribution-specific rules for risk, evidence, accountability, and review gates. It introduces the Agent Governance Manifest (AGM), a repository-hosted, bidirectional governance contract that assigns evidence preparation to contributors and agents while retaining maintainer verification and acceptance authority. A 50-repository audit and two controlled evaluations provide early evidence that structured AGM materials can improve reviewers' recovery of governance state and contributors' preparation of evidence packages.

## Key claims
- The authors distinguish agent-readability, which makes repository context accessible to agents, and traceability, which records AI participation, from governability: project-side infrastructure that makes a specific contribution risk-classifiable, evidence-supported, human-accountable, and reviewable.
- In a diagnostic audit of 50 GitHub repositories, general OSS governance artifacts and agent-readable materials were common but AI-governance cues were fragmented; no repository provided a project-wide arrangement combining shared rules, preparation obligations, verification rights, and maintainer decision authority.
- In a within-participant reviewer-side evaluation with 15 participants and 75 task-level outputs, AGM-supported materials improved exact risk-label recovery from 15/37 to 37/38 and perceived review support from 3.27 to 6.14 on a 1–7 scale.
- In a contributor-side feasibility check with 15 participants completing 45 tasks, all final AGM-supported packages represented the core governance state correctly and 41 passed strict structural validation.

## Evidence & limitations
- The paper combines a descriptive audit of public GitHub repositories with controlled reviewer-side and contributor-side evaluations; its evidence concerns structured governance-state recovery and package preparation, not field outcomes such as code quality, security, maintainer workload, adoption, or long-run community legitimacy.
- AGM is a proposed governance artifact under journal review, so its framework and controlled results should not be treated as validated deployment practice across OSS communities or other agentic work settings.
