# Work design

Work design concerns the characteristics through which tasks, knowledge demands, relationships, and organizational context structure a job and shape worker outcomes. [[2023-parker-smart-work-design]] organizes this broad terrain with the SMART model: stimulating, mastery, autonomous, relational, and tolerable characteristics form a higher-order architecture rather than a loose inventory of job features.

The SMART categories group cognitive challenge and variety; clarity and feedback; discretion over decisions, timing, and methods; social support, task significance, and beneficiary contact; and the absence of overload, work–home conflict, and role conflict. [[2023-parker-smart-work-design]] This architecture makes work design a distinct analytic question from AI-specific workflow redesign: task allocation can change a workflow, but assessing its likely worker consequences requires identifying which broader work characteristics it preserves, expands, or degrades.

Across two samples, the proposed five-factor higher-order structure fit better than alternatives, and longitudinal analyses linked SMART dimensions to later job satisfaction directly or through challenge appraisal, meaningfulness, relatedness-need fulfillment, and activated negative affect. [[2023-parker-smart-work-design]] A third study associated relevant employee-rated SMART characteristics with leader-rated performance. [[2023-parker-smart-work-design]] The framework therefore offers a common language for examining how work arrangements shape outcomes, rather than assuming that a technically efficient task configuration is necessarily good work.

## Connections
- Connects to [[work-redesign]] because SMART provides the task, knowledge, social, and contextual characteristic architecture for assessing what AI-specific workflow reallocation preserves, expands, or degrades. [[2023-parker-smart-work-design]]
- Connects to [[ai-mediated-work-experience]] because autonomy, feedback, meaningfulness, social support, conflict, and overload are work-characteristic pathways associated with satisfaction and performance. [[2023-parker-smart-work-design]]

## Contradictions & open questions
- [[2023-parker-smart-work-design]] supports the SMART structure and its associations with satisfaction and performance, but its observational and longitudinal designs do not establish that changing a SMART characteristic through an intervention causes those outcomes.
- [[2023-parker-smart-work-design]] does not test AI-enabled work arrangements. It remains open whether AI-driven task reallocation changes SMART characteristics in the same ways as conventional work-design interventions, and whether the model captures AI-specific issues such as algorithmic control, verification burden, and human–AI coordination.
