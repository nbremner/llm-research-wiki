# AI-mediated organizational networks

AI-mediated organizational networks tracks how AI changes the human-human interaction structure of work: who collaborates, who becomes a knowledge source, which ties become cheaper, and where new coordination burdens appear. The point is not only that AI helps individuals perform tasks; it is that AI can alter the social graph through which organizations move knowledge, translate expertise, and coordinate work. [[2026-buechsenschuss-genai-organizational-networks]] [[2026-shi-when-bots-join-the-team]]

[[2007-serenko-organizational-size-knowledge-flow]] gives this topic a pre-AI baseline: organizational units tend to lose internal knowledge flow as they grow because formal structure becomes more complex, interpersonal trust weakens, communication becomes less frequent, and employees doubt that shared knowledge will reach the people who need it. That makes AI-mediated networks theoretically important: if grounded GenAI lowers search, translation, routing, and connective-efficacy costs, it could weaken a classic size penalty rather than merely speeding up individual knowledge work. [[2007-serenko-organizational-size-knowledge-flow]] [[2026-buechsenschuss-genai-organizational-networks]]

[[2026-buechsenschuss-genai-organizational-networks]] gives the wiki its first direct field-experimental evidence for this network-rewiring claim. In a European technology services firm, 42 teams were randomized to receive a grounded GenAI assistant or continue without it; after three months, employees with access to the tool had larger increases in collaboration-network and knowledge-sharing-network centrality, as well as higher satisfaction with knowledge access and more projects executed. That shifts the adoption question from “did workers use AI?” to “did AI change the pattern of human dependence and exchange around the work?” [[2026-buechsenschuss-genai-organizational-networks]]

The source’s mechanism is useful for work design: grounded GenAI can act as a translator, lowering the cost of collaboration across expertise boundaries, and as a knowledge catalyst, making AI-augmented employees more valuable sources for others. That means AI can increase human-human ties rather than simply replacing them. It also means organizational value may appear as changed brokerage, centrality, and knowledge flow before it appears as a clean job-level automation or substitution outcome. [[2026-buechsenschuss-genai-organizational-networks]]

Role differences matter. [[2026-buechsenschuss-genai-organizational-networks]] found specialists gained more in knowledge-network in-degree than generalists, while generalists gained more in satisfaction and project output. In I-O terms, the same AI resource may change different roles through different pathways: for specialists, by making deep expertise more accessible and sought after; for generalists, by improving coordination and throughput across a broader project portfolio. [[2026-buechsenschuss-genai-organizational-networks]]

[[2025-hautamaki-fully-leveraging-ai-b2b-sales]] adds qualitative evidence on the managerial work behind AI-mediated knowledge flow. In B2B sales organizations, managers used social capital to make data a common language, frame AI as an analytical collaborator, surface use cases in team routines, and connect sales with IT, data, HR, finance, and subject-matter experts. That is not network measurement evidence, but it explains a plausible management mechanism by which AI changes who needs to collaborate with whom. [[2025-hautamaki-fully-leveraging-ai-b2b-sales]]

[[2026-shi-when-bots-join-the-team]] extends the network lens beyond individual AI assistance to rule-based bots that participate visibly in a project's shared interaction record. Across 2,991 open-source software projects, first bot adoption was associated with more repeated human engagement and bot-directed social memory over 12-month pre/post windows, suggesting that bots can become part of the coordination infrastructure through which contributors sustain relationships and remember prior interaction. [[2026-shi-when-bots-join-the-team]]

The same study associated bot adoption with lower conflict-cascade rates and more distinctive project output, while joint models linked repeated engagement, bot-to-human addressing, and role differentiation to fewer cascades. The attenuation of the bot-intensity association with conflict after those organizational variables were included is consistent with a network-mediated account, but it does not establish mediation or a causal effect of bot adoption. [[2026-shi-when-bots-join-the-team]]

Together, the field experiment and the open-source event study indicate that AI-mediated network change can take different forms: grounded GenAI may increase employees' human collaboration and knowledge-sharing centrality, whereas predictable project bots may reinforce repeated engagement, social memory, and conflict-management routines in a shared digital institution. [[2026-buechsenschuss-genai-organizational-networks]] [[2026-shi-when-bots-join-the-team]]

## Connections
- Connects to [[ai-mediated-teamwork]] because team benefits and network benefits are adjacent but not identical: teams are bounded units, while organizational networks capture emergent ties across roles, groups, and expertise boundaries. [[2026-buechsenschuss-genai-organizational-networks]] [[2026-shi-when-bots-join-the-team]]
- Connects to [[work-redesign]] because AI can reorganize collaboration and knowledge-flow architecture without formally changing job descriptions first, and because unit size and cross-unit exchange are design variables when organizations try to preserve knowledge flow at scale. [[2007-serenko-organizational-size-knowledge-flow]] [[2026-buechsenschuss-genai-organizational-networks]]
- Connects to [[human-ai-collaboration]] because the human-AI interface can change the value and frequency of subsequent human-human collaboration, including through a bot's visible participation in shared project interaction. [[2026-buechsenschuss-genai-organizational-networks]] [[2026-shi-when-bots-join-the-team]]
- Connects to [[ai-mediated-work-experience]] because greater knowledge access and network centrality can coincide with satisfaction, while the consequences of being a more central knowledge source still require direct evidence. [[2026-buechsenschuss-genai-organizational-networks]]
- Connects to [[ai-workforce-impact-measurement]] because productivity-only impact measures can miss changes in network centrality, knowledge access, interaction structure, conflict cascades, and output distinctiveness. [[2026-buechsenschuss-genai-organizational-networks]] [[2026-shi-when-bots-join-the-team]]
- Connects to [[ai-adoption]] and [[ai-readiness]] because cross-functional knowledge transfer and shared use-case learning can support AI adoption, while bot adoption can also become embedded in a project's interaction routines. [[2025-hautamaki-fully-leveraging-ai-b2b-sales]] [[2026-shi-when-bots-join-the-team]]

## Contradictions & open questions
- [[2026-buechsenschuss-genai-organizational-networks]] supports network expansion after grounded GenAI introduction, but its network outcomes are self-reported and observed in one firm over three months; digital-trace and longer-term evidence are needed before treating network rewiring as a general adoption effect. [[2026-buechsenschuss-genai-organizational-networks]]
- [[2026-shi-when-bots-join-the-team]] reports stronger repeated engagement, social memory, lower conflict cascades, and more distinctive output around bot adoption, but the study has no untreated comparison group and explicitly does not make causal or mediation claims; its associations should not be equated with the randomized GenAI effects in [[2026-buechsenschuss-genai-organizational-networks]]. [[2026-shi-when-bots-join-the-team]] [[2026-buechsenschuss-genai-organizational-networks]]
- [[2007-serenko-organizational-size-knowledge-flow]] is a conceptual pre-AI theory rather than a direct test, so the useful question is not whether the 150-person threshold still holds literally; it is whether AI changes the mechanisms that made size costly: trust, communication frequency, connective efficacy, routing, and cross-unit reach. [[2007-serenko-organizational-size-knowledge-flow]]
- Increased centrality, repeated engagement, or bot-directed social memory are not automatically good outcomes; the available studies do not yet show whether they create durable coordination gains or new interruption, overload, and concentration burdens for people who become more central. [[2026-buechsenschuss-genai-organizational-networks]] [[2026-shi-when-bots-join-the-team]]
- The source’s role comparison is theoretically useful but not causal because employees were not randomly assigned to specialist versus generalist roles; role-based redesign claims should stay tentative until tested across organizations and task structures. [[2026-buechsenschuss-genai-organizational-networks]]
- [[2025-hautamaki-fully-leveraging-ai-b2b-sales]] gives manager-reported mechanisms for cross-functional AI knowledge transfer, but not observed network data. It should be read as a qualitative mechanism source, not as evidence that AI actually increased network centrality, tie strength, or collaboration volume. [[2025-hautamaki-fully-leveraging-ai-b2b-sales]]
