# Agentic organization design

I use agentic organization design to mean the architecture that differentiates interdependent agent work, routes context, preserves evidence, coordinates tools and memory, validates intermediate outputs, and defines permissions and escalation points before outputs enter accountable human work; this is distinct from treating an agent collective as either one model or a literal human team. [[2026-liu-organizational-behavior-agentic-ai]] [[2026-gao-making-agent-mediated-contributions-governable]]

[[2026-liu-organizational-behavior-agentic-ai]] frames agent collectives as a partial organizational analogue: they can display differentiation, interdependence, recurrent routines, boundary crossing, and collective outcomes, but they lack the motivation, identity, trust, socialization, and moral accountability that make human organizations social orders. [[2026-liu-organizational-behavior-agentic-ai]] I therefore treat prompts, schemas, shared memory, traces, tools, validators, and permissions as the coordination substrate, rather than importing committees or hierarchies merely because their names sound organizational. [[2026-liu-organizational-behavior-agentic-ai]]

Liu's proposed contextual transaction cost makes the topology trade-off explicit: handoffs can add latency and token cost, compression loss, semantic drift, verification work, and governance burden. [[2026-liu-organizational-behavior-agentic-ai]] In its 8,000-task synthetic simulation, shared-state blackboard and adaptive forms outperformed a single-expert baseline on collective efficiency, quality, and success probability, while pipeline, hierarchy, and committee forms were negative on the main outcomes. [[2026-liu-organizational-behavior-agentic-ai]] The paper's trace-instrumented LLM runs qualify a universal prescription because shared-state and adaptive designs sometimes improved quality while incurring higher contextual cost. [[2026-liu-organizational-behavior-agentic-ai]] More agents are consequently worth their coordination burden only when their work is independent, complementary, or verifiable and the necessary context remains durable and inspectable. [[2026-liu-organizational-behavior-agentic-ai]]

Gao et al. sharpen the governance part of that architecture for open-source contribution workflows: agent-readable instructions and provenance traces alone do not make a contribution governable, because projects also need contribution-specific risk classification, evidence requirements, accountability assignments, and review gates. [[2026-gao-making-agent-mediated-contributions-governable]] Their proposed Agent Governance Manifest assigns evidence preparation to contributors and agents while retaining maintainer verification and acceptance authority, making the human accountability boundary an explicit design element rather than a residual manual check. [[2026-gao-making-agent-mediated-contributions-governable]]

The early evaluation suggests that structured governance artifacts can reduce ambiguity at the handoff: in Gao et al.'s reviewer study, AGM-supported materials improved exact risk-label recovery from 15/37 to 37/38 and raised perceived review support from 3.27 to 6.14 on a seven-point scale; in its contributor feasibility check, all final AGM-supported packages represented the core governance state correctly and 41 of 45 passed strict structural validation. [[2026-gao-making-agent-mediated-contributions-governable]] I read these results as evidence for inspectable evidence packages and decision rights, not as proof that one manifest or topology improves deployed code quality, security, maintainer workload, adoption, or community legitimacy. [[2026-gao-making-agent-mediated-contributions-governable]]

The human-facing counterpart is an interface organization: a workflow arrangement specifying which outputs may cross into accountable work, which evidence and uncertainty must travel with them, what agents may access or do, which traces are retained, and where a human must exercise judgment. [[2026-liu-organizational-behavior-agentic-ai]] [[2026-gao-making-agent-mediated-contributions-governable]] In high-consequence work, an opaque, fragile, or expensive-to-verify output can be organizationally worse than a lower-friction workflow with clear evidence and recoverable accountability. [[2026-liu-organizational-behavior-agentic-ai]] [[2026-gao-making-agent-mediated-contributions-governable]]

## Contradictions & open questions
- [[2026-liu-organizational-behavior-agentic-ai]] finds gains for shared-state and adaptive forms in synthetic tasks but observes higher contextual costs in real LLM traces; the relevant comparison is task-contingent collective value, not a general preference for multi-agent complexity. [[2026-liu-organizational-behavior-agentic-ai]]
- [[2026-liu-organizational-behavior-agentic-ai]] cautions that human-imitation forms can create lossy handoffs and correlated deliberation, but this does not establish that human organizational forms are generally poor models for agent systems; it establishes a mechanism to test when their functional features transfer without their social foundations. [[2026-liu-organizational-behavior-agentic-ai]]
- Gao et al.'s controlled evidence supports governance-state recovery and evidence-package preparation, while it does not measure field outcomes such as code quality, security, maintainer workload, adoption, or long-run community legitimacy; whether its proposed governance contract improves those outcomes remains open. [[2026-gao-making-agent-mediated-contributions-governable]]
- The evidence remains computational theorizing, benchmark-like traces, repository audit, and controlled task evaluation rather than field evidence from durable human-agent workflows; it remains open whether context architecture, trace design, permission scope, evidence obligations, and escalation rules predict output quality, recovery, worker experience, accountability, and organizational value in deployed settings. [[2026-liu-organizational-behavior-agentic-ai]] [[2026-gao-making-agent-mediated-contributions-governable]]
